**Why the split instead of putting all four rules in `normalizeCrawlUrl`:** that function feeds three things — the URL the crawler **fetches**, the **crawl-scope** derivation, and the document **identity**. Host-case and tracking params are safe to strip everywhere. A trailing slash is *resource-significant*, so stripping it inside `normalizeCrawlUrl` would have caused two regressions:

- **Fetch**: a `/docs/` start URL would be requested as `/docs` → 404 on any server that doesn't redirect (every test fixture does exact-match: `web-crawler-connector.test.ts:724-731`).
- **Scope**: `buildAllowedPathPrefixes` (`:472`) derives `[expr]` from `/docs/` but `[expr]` (the whole site) from `/docs` — a silent scope broadening.

Applying the trailing-slash strip at the identity layer achieves "canonicalize to one" (verified: `/docs/install/`, `/docs/install`, and `[expr]` variants all collapse to one `id`/`sourceUrl`) while the crawler still fetches `/docs/` and still bounds the crawl to `/docs/`.

Pages with clean URLs are unaffected (matched and updated in place). So indexed documents keep being served, but affected ones become duplicated — **the change is not backward-complete on its own.**
